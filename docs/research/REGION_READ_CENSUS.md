## What a read bounds, and the null each family beats

A read's offset inside the row is what decides whether a layout follows. A row of many words
whose readers touch a handful of offsets is not a layout: it is those offsets, and the ratio
below is what the concentration is worth. A row the reads cover completely is an element array,
and the question there is the element count and the element's meaning.

| family | rows | bytes | median share of the row covered | expected words per offset read | address words of the family |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `copy` | 12 | 3478 | 0.50 | 2.0 | 0 of 0 |
| `fragment` | 90 | 180 | 1.00 | 1.0 | 0 of 0 |
| `array` | 37 | 180 | 1.00 | 1.0 | 29 of 43 |
| `concentration` | 126 | 53221 | 0.25 | 2.4 | 778 of 13289 |

### The instructions behind three of those rows

A `copy` row, quoted from the call site the sweep names instead of the routine:

    0x08009B2C  3ff0f8fa  bl #0x8049120

The return address is `0x08009B31`, the read pc, and the routine at `0x08049120` is the
copy. The row's bytes reached the caller and no field was read.

A `concentration` row's reader, quoted whole, from the object search at `0x08004A9E`:

    0x08004AA2  6f69  ldr r7, [r5, #0x14]
    0x08004AAC  9b69  ldr r3, [r3, #0x18]
    0x08004AAE  1e78  ldrb r6, [r3]
    0x08004AB0  8e42  cmp r6, r1
    0x08004AB4  5e78  ldrb r6, [r3, #1]
    0x08004AB6  9642  cmp r6, r2
    0x08004ABA  5888  ldrh r0, [r3, #2]

The pointer at `+0x18` is indexed by the count read at `+0x14`, so the base of these loads
is an array element chosen at run time: the three offsets are the element's fields and the
row is whatever lies around that element. That is the shape the family's median share of
the row states, and it is why the offsets do not add up to a layout.

A `concentration` row's other reader, quoted from `0x0800C378`:

    0x0800C380  1206  lsls r2, r2, #0x18
    0x0800C382  920d  lsrs r2, r2, #0x16
    0x0800C384  9a58  ldr r2, [r3, r2]

The index is a byte field shifted left by 24 and back by 22, so it is a multiple of four
taken from that byte and the load is a table entry at that offset. Which entries a run
touches depends on the byte's value, not on the row.

### `copy`: the copy routine read the row and no field did

A copy return is the address after a `bl` into the routine, so the row's bytes reached the
caller's buffer and no instruction read a field of them. The null is the copy itself: it
states a source pointer and a length the caller chose, never a field or a count inside the
row. A row stays raw until code that reads the copied bytes is measured.

### `fragment`: the read is wider than the row

A read wider than the row crosses the row's boundary, so the instruction reads the row and its
neighbour as one word. No layout inside the row carries that word: its other bytes lie in the
neighbouring carrier row, which is the rule P-847 records for a split row.

### `array`: the reads cover every byte of the row

37 rows / 180 B are covered completely, at their own offsets. That is a layout of words, and
what it does not state is the element count: the reads are one indexed load per element whose
index is a runtime value masked out of a byte field (`lsls`, then `lsrs` by the field's own
width), and no compare in the reader bounds the index. The count the reads do support is the
row's own length over four, and a layout built on that alone is refused here.
Of the family's 43 words, 29 are an address in the image; the rest are numbers, and a `DCD`
of numbers is a dump of retail bytes, which the route vocabulary refuses
(`docs/research/ROUTE_TO_100.md`, `## Route vocabulary`). 26 row(s) of the family hold only
address words, and such a row is a pointer row: it converts when a tracked symbol names its
target, which is the null `docs/research/POINTER_ROWS.csv` records for the same shape. The
falsifier for the rest is a count instruction in the reader, or a symbol naming every word.

### `concentration`: the reader states offsets, not the row

126 rows / 53221 B hold many words and their readers read few offsets of them. A row whose
readers name a handful of offsets holds hundreds of words; the same number of reads over a
uniformly random row lands on that many offsets by construction. What a
field load does state is the offset and the width it used; what it never states is where the
row ends.

## Rows that carry the `pending-image-route` status

38 rows / 2716 B of the ten regions are a `graphics` row or the map block's 24 B carve-out
row `0x29B308`, and they carry the status `pending-image-route`: the animation row's payload is a JCALG1 stream decoded at IWRAM 0x03000040 before the copy to VRAM, or it is the carve-out 0x29B308; not carried by the image route (docs/research/MAP_BLOCK_LAYOUTS.md).
The rule is the census class, not the reader: the retail bytes are a compressed stream the
decoder at IWRAM `0x03000040` expands, and `0x08049138` copies the decoded buffer to VRAM
(`docs/research/MAP_BLOCK_LAYOUTS.md`). The image round trip carries the 125 sections in
`tools/mapcontainer/animframe_manifest.csv`; the rows here are the ones it cannot carry - a
row holding no frame, a frame whose extent crosses a carrier token, the refused row, and the
carve-out - each measured in that page.

## What converted

No family above bounds both a layout and an extent, so every row of the ten regions keeps
its census null, now carrying the sweep's verdict; the `graphics` rows carry
`pending-image-route` instead. The largest null family is
`concentration` at 126 rows / 53221 B, and the reason it does not convert is a measurement, not a
gap: those readers touch between 1 and 8 offsets of rows of up to 832 words. The `array`
family is fully read and still refuses for the two reasons stated above, and the `copy` and
`fragment` families state no field at all.
